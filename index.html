Mutual Funds Terms

Everything you need to know about how mutual funds operate in the Indian stock market, key risk-return metrics, and how distributor commissions affect your wealth.

What Exactly is a Mutual Fund?

A Mutual Fund is a financial vehicle where money from thousands of retail and institutional investors is pooled together. This consolidated capital (called the AUM) is managed by a licensed Asset Management Company (AMC) like SBI, HDFC, ICICI Prudential, or Nippon India. A professional Fund Manager and their research team invest this pool into a diversified basket of equities (NSE/BSE stocks), debt securities (government bonds, commercial paper), or money market instruments in accordance with the scheme's declared investment objective.

👥
1. Investors Pool Money
Thousands of investors contribute as little as ₹500 via SIP or Lumpsum.
➔
🏢
2. AMC & Fund Manager
SEBI-registered fund manager analyzes earnings, moats & valuations.
➔
📈
3. Diversified Portfolio
Invests in 40–80 top NSE/BSE companies (Reliance, HDFC Bank, TCS, etc.).
➔
💰
4. NAV Units & Returns
Investors receive proportional units at the daily Net Asset Value (NAV).
🛡️

Instant Diversification

Buying individual shares of 40 top Indian stocks requires lakhs of rupees. In a mutual fund, even a ₹500 SIP gives you instant fractional exposure across multiple market sectors (banking, IT, pharma, auto).

🧠

Professional Management

Full-time CFAs and equity analysts track corporate quarterly earnings, management governance, macro tailwinds, and portfolio risk so you don't have to watch ticker screens all day.

💧

High Liquidity & Transparency

Open-ended mutual funds can be bought or redeemed anytime on business days at the day's declared NAV. Portfolios and fund fact-sheets are published monthly per AMFI regulations.

🏛️

SEBI Regulated & Safe

Mutual funds in India are strictly regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Investor funds are held by an independent third-party custodian (not the fund manager directly).

SEBI Equity Categorization in Indian Markets

Large Cap Funds
Must invest at least 80% in Top 100 Indian companies by market capitalization (e.g. Reliance, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank). Known for stability and steady long-term compounding.
Mid Cap Funds
Must invest at least 65% in 101st to 250th companies (e.g. Persistent, Trent, Dixon). High growth potential with moderate to high volatility.
Small Cap Funds
Must invest at least 65% in 251st company downwards. Highest upside potential over 7–10+ year horizons, but experiences sharper short-term market drawdowns.
Flexi Cap Funds
Dynamic mandate investing at least 65% in Indian equities across Large, Mid, and Small caps with complete fund manager freedom to allocate where valuations are attractive.
ELSS (Tax Saver)
Equity Linked Savings Scheme qualifying for Section 80C tax deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakh. Comes with a statutory 3-year lock-in period (shortest among 80C tax instruments).
Index Funds
Passive funds mirroring benchmark indices like NIFTY 50 or SENSEX with identical stock weights. Ultra-low expense ratios (0.1% to 0.2%) with zero human manager bias.

Showing 10 Essential Metrics
PRICING

NAV (Net Asset Value)

The market price of a single mutual fund unit. It is calculated by the AMC at the end of every trading day (by 11:00 PM per AMFI norms) after deducting management expenses from the total portfolio value.

Formula: NAV = (Total Market Value of Assets - Liabilities & Expenses) ÷ Total Units Issued
Example: If a fund's NAV is ₹100 and you invest ₹10,000, you are allotted exactly 100 units. If the NAV grows to ₹120, your portfolio is worth ₹12,000.
RETURNS

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

The true annualized geometric growth rate that measures an investment's return over multiple years, dampening the effect of annual volatility. Absolute returns are misleading for horizons longer than 1 year.

Formula: CAGR = [(Ending Value ÷ Beginning Value) ^ (1 ÷ Years)] - 1
Example: If ₹1,00,000 becomes ₹2,00,000 in 5 years, the absolute return is 100%, but the true CAGR is 14.87% per annum.
COST

Expense Ratio (TER)

The annual percentage fee charged by the AMC to cover fund manager salaries, operational costs, compliance, and distribution. It is deducted daily on a pro-rata basis directly from the NAV before returns are posted.

Rule of Thumb: Lower is always better. Direct plans have an expense ratio 0.5%–1.2% lower than Regular plans for the exact same portfolio!
Example: If a fund earns 16% gross return and has a 0.60% TER, your net return credited to the NAV is 15.40%.
FUND SIZE

AUM (Assets Under Management)

The cumulative market value of all investor capital currently managed under the scheme (in ₹ Crores).

Impact: High AUM indicates strong investor trust and liquidity. However, in Small Cap funds, massive AUM (>₹40,000 Cr) can sometimes make it harder for the manager to enter/exit illiquid micro-caps.
Example: Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund manages over ₹84,000 Cr in AUM across retail and institutional Indian investors.
OUTPERFORMANCE

Alpha (α)

Measures the value-add or excess return generated by the active fund manager above and beyond what the underlying benchmark index delivered.

Benchmark Standard: Positive Alpha (>0) = Manager beat the market; Negative Alpha (<0) = Fund underperformed the index.
Example: If NIFTY 50 returns 15% and an active Large Cap fund returns 19.5% with a Beta of 1, the fund manager generated an Alpha of +4.5%.
VOLATILITY

Beta (β)

Measures how sensitive a mutual fund's price movements are in comparison to the benchmark index (which has a baseline Beta of 1.0).

Interpretation: Beta < 1 = Less volatile than the market (defensive); Beta > 1 = More volatile than the market (aggressive).
Example: A fund with a Beta of 0.80 will generally fall only 8% if the market drops 10%, offering superior downside protection during crashes.
RISK-ADJUSTED

Sharpe & Sortino Ratios

Evaluate whether a fund's high returns are coming from smart management or reckless risk-taking. Sharpe divides excess return by total standard deviation; Sortino penalizes only harmful downside volatility.

Higher is Better: A Sharpe Ratio > 1.3 is considered good in Indian equity mutual funds; > 1.6 is exceptional.
Example: Between two funds returning 25%, Fund A (Sharpe 1.7) delivered that return with much smoother ride than Fund B (Sharpe 0.9).
INVESTING METHOD

SIP, Step-Up SIP & Lumpsum

SIP invests a fixed amount every month, enabling Rupee Cost Averaging (buying more units when markets drop, fewer when expensive). Step-Up SIP increases the monthly amount by 10%–15% each year as your salary increases. Lumpsum invests a one-time single capital block.

Why Step-Up SIP: Increasing your SIP by just 10% each year can nearly double your 15-year maturity corpus!
Example: ₹10,000/mo regular SIP for 15Y at 15% = ₹67 Lakhs. A 10% annual Step-Up SIP creates ₹1.15 Crores with the same starting amount.
PENALTY

Exit Load & Lock-in

A fractional fee (typically 1%) charged by the AMC if you redeem units before a minimum holding tenure (usually 365 days) to discourage short-term speculation. ELSS funds have zero exit load but have a mandatory 3-year statutory lock-in.

Note: Exit loads collected go back into the fund pool to protect long-term investors from transaction costs caused by redeemers.
TAXATION (UNION BUDGET)

LTCG & STCG Taxation (India)

Tax rules applicable when redeeming equity mutual fund units:

• LTCG (Long Term Capital Gains): Holding period > 12 months. Taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding the ₹1.25 Lakh exemption per financial year.
• STCG (Short Term Capital Gains): Holding period ≤ 12 months. Taxed at flat 20%.
Example: If your long-term capital gain after 3 years is ₹3,00,000, the first ₹1,25,000 is tax-free. You pay 12.5% tax only on the remaining ₹1,75,000 = ₹21,875.
THE CRITICAL DIFFERENCE

Direct Plan vs. Regular Plan — Don't Lose Lakhs to Commissions

Since January 2013, SEBI mandated that every mutual fund scheme in India must offer two variants: a Direct Plan and a Regular Plan. Both invest in the exact same underlying portfolio and are managed by the exact same fund manager. The only difference is distributor commission.

🌱

Direct Plan (Recommended)

You invest directly with the AMC (or through modern direct execution platforms). There is zero distributor or broker commission.

  • Expense Ratio: 0.3% – 0.8%
  • Commission to Agents: ₹0 (0%)
  • Daily NAV: Consistently higher
  • 15-Year Wealth: Up to 15%–25% higher total corpus
⚠️

Regular Plan (Commission Drag)

You invest through a bank RM, distributor, agent, or traditional broker. The AMC silently deducts a 0.5%–1.5% recurring annual commission from your daily NAV every day and pays it to the agent.

  • Expense Ratio: 1.3% – 2.2%
  • Commission to Agents: 0.5% – 1.5% paid every year for life!
  • Daily NAV: Lower every day due to commission leakage
  • Deduction: Deducted even when the market is crashing
